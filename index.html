About Sia

❦
Shivanshi “Sia” Thakur

Shivanshi — lovingly known as Sia — is a devoted student of the classical Indian dance form Bharatanatyam.

For years, Sia has dedicated herself to the grammar of this ancient art: the precise footwork (adavus), the eloquent hand gestures (mudras), the storytelling of the eyes and face (abhinaya), and the rhythmic soul of the music. What began as a childhood curiosity has blossomed into a lifelong passion.

Her Arangetram marks the culmination of this rigorous training — the moment a student steps onto the stage, alone, to offer everything she has learned. It is at once a graduation, a prayer, and a celebration.

“To dance is to pray with the whole body.”
✦ The Meaning

What is an Arangetram?

❦

Ascending the Stage

The word Arangetram comes from Tamil — arangu (stage) and etram (ascending). It is a dancer’s first full solo performance before an audience.

Years of Devotion

It represents many years of disciplined training under a Guru — a rite of passage where the student demonstrates mastery of technique, rhythm and expression.

A Solo Journey

Across a full-length recital, the dancer performs alone, moving through a traditional repertoire that builds from pure rhythm to deep emotional storytelling.

The Programme

Sia’s Arangetram unfolds through the following pieces, from the opening offering to the rhythmic finale.

  1. Pushpanjali & Alarippu — A flower offering of reverence, followed by a rhythmic opening that gradually brings the eyes, neck, hands and body into movement.
  2. Jatiswaram — Pure dance set to musical notes, weaving precise footwork and graceful movement into intricate rhythmic patterns.
  3. Shabdam — A lyrical piece that introduces storytelling through expressive gestures, facial expression and movement.
  4. Varnam — The centrepiece of the recital, combining demanding rhythmic sequences with expressive storytelling and emotion.
  5. Mahishasura Mardini — A tribute to Goddess Durga and her triumph over Mahishasura, celebrating courage, divine strength and the victory of good over evil.
  6. Natyamanjari — A celebration of dance through rhythm, movement and expression.
  7. Own composition: surprise — An original creation by Sia, kept as a surprise to be revealed on stage.
  8. Thillana — A vibrant finale of brisk footwork, sculptural poses and joyful rhythm.

The Guru

❦

Dr. Sanjukta Banerjee

Guru · Founder & Artistic Director, Saugandhikam Dance Academy

Dr. Sanjukta Banerjee is an Indo-Canadian classical dancer, choreographer and educator whose practice spans Bharatanatyam, Mohiniyattam and Kathakali. She holds a PhD in Dance from York University and trained at Kolkata Kalamandalam under Kalamandalam P. Govindan Kutty and Dr. Thankamani Kutty.

Many know her as Goddess Durga in Doordarshan’s Mahalaya presentation of Mahishasura Mardini. Alongside her television work, she has brought Indian classical dance to international audiences through performances, workshops and lecture-demonstrations.

As founder and artistic director of Saugandhikam Dance Academy, she nurtures dancers across the Greater Toronto Area. Sia’s Arangetram is a celebration of this guru–shishya bond, with heartfelt gratitude for her Guru’s guidance, care and dedication.
